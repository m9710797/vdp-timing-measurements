# IKA9958: what a die-shot reverse engineering of the V9958 tells us about VRAM timing

This document was written with assistance from an AI coding agent.

A side investigation, kept separate from FINDINGS7 on purpose. Nothing here has
been folded into the model or into `fit_2026.cc`; the one experiment that touches
`fit_2026` was run entirely through existing command-line flags.

Source: <https://github.com/ika-musume/IKA9958>, "A BSD-licensed core for
Yamaha's V9958 (c) 2024 Sehyeon Kim (Raki)", BSD-2-Clause. Found via a link in
the discussion on openMSX PR #2191. Inspected at commit `1c813e27`
(2026-09-06, the most recent at the time of writing).

Two headline results.

1. **The access-slot lattice is in the published Verilog and it reproduces our
   three measured tables** — line length 1368 exactly, slot counts 154/88/31
   exactly, every position within one `phiL` tick, the sprites-on active-display
   interior exact.
2. **The arbiter is not in the Verilog but it is traced in the KiCad schematics**,
   as a closed 54-cell cone. Simulated, it says the grant rule is *the first
   CPU-legal slot at or after a fixed delay from the `/CSx` rising edge*, that
   the CPU-legal slots are exactly the ones our `cpu_slots_of` picks (agreeing on
   25 of 25 suppressions), that the buffer is one bit, and that **the decision
   rule has no display-mode dependence at all**.

**Correction to an earlier revision:** a fitted sprite-coincident `THRESH`
term was suspicious because CI did not produce it. It turned out to compensate
for comparing raw wall-clock distance at RCC-padded row 1330 with the stalled
`phiL` distance used by the arbiter. With one signed engine-distance metric, CI
gives the complete rule and the same 263/266 shared corpus result; no sprite
term remains. Section §4f records both the superseded fit and its resolution.

## 2. The Memory PLA is the access-slot lattice

`IKA9958_pla.sv` ends with a section labelled "Memory PLA" that produces four
signals, registered on `phiL_NCEN`:

```
PLA.vram_ras_rq[1] <= |mpla[19:15] | |mpla[11:3] | |mpla[1:0];        //gt107
PLA.vram_ras_rq[0] <= |mpla[22:20] | mpla[18] | |mpla[14:12]
                    | |mpla[9:8] | mpla[6] | |mpla[2:1] | mpla[-1];   //gt109
PLA.vram_cpu_slot  <= |mpla[29:25] & ~|bpla[85:84];                   //gt108
PLA.vram_spr_slot  <= mpla[24];
```

`vram_cpu_slot` is our slot lattice. The comment above it reads "These signals
generate the VRAM CPU slot strobe without any additional conditions", and for
graphics modes there are three separate terms:

```
mpla[27] = M inside {G4..G7} && av_gmode_w__spr && !gt076;             // sprites on
mpla[28] = M inside {G4..G7} && av_gmode_wo_spr && !gt077;             // sprites off
mpla[29] = M inside {G4..G7} && !av_gmode_common && !dg_act && !gt078; // display off
```

with

```
gt076 = ~gt072 & ~bpla[47] & ~bpla[48] & ~bpla[49];
gt077 = (gt059 | ~bpla[44]) & (bpc[0] | gt063) & ~gt072 & ~bpla[48];
gt078 = (bpc[0] | gt063) & (bpc[0] | gt064 | bpla_z[43]);
```

That is a one-to-one structural match with `SLOTS_SPRON`, `SLOTS_SPROFF` and
`SLOTS_DISPOFF`. The `bpc[0]` term in `gt077`/`gt078` is why display off has an
8-cycle pitch: `bpc` is clocked at `phiL` = one tick per 4 VDP cycles, so a term
gated on `bpc[0]` can only fire every other tick.

`mpla[24]` (`vram_spr_slot`) is the sprite VRAM slot. The README's "Sprite logic
not started" refers to sprite rendering, not the slot, so sprite slot timing is
available too. Whether `dg_act` (the R#0 DG digitize bit) suppresses slots is
also spelled out, which we have never measured.

### 2.1 The counter, and where 1368 comes from

`bpc` is 9 bits: `bpc[8:4]` is a tile index, `bpc[3:0]` a pixel index within the
tile, clocked at `phiL`. Its trajectory is not a plain wrap. Two decodes reload
it, each with one register stage of delay, and a third reload comes from the
screen-timing block:

* `bpla[2]` (`bpc == 0x1F6`) reloads to `0x000`
* `bpla[6]` (`bpc == 0x12F`) reloads to `0x1D1`, via `gt042`/`gt033`
* `ST.hadd_eq23` (`hcntr + R#18.H == 23`) reloads to `0x1E3`

Free-running with `hadd_eq23` held low, the period is

```
0x000..0x00F   16 ticks
0x010..0x12F  288
0x130           1   (the reload's register delay)
0x1D1..0x1DF   15
0x1E0..0x1EF   16
0x1F0..0x1F6    7
0x1F7           1   (ditto)
              ---
              344 ticks = 1376 VDP cycles
```

The H-adjust reload is therefore not cosmetic: it truncates the line by two
`phiL` ticks to the real **342 ticks = 1368 cycles**. Firing `hadd_eq23` when
`bpc == 0x1DF` gives exactly 1368; that is the one parameter fitted rather than
derived, because the published `hcntr` is wrong (§5).

### 2.2 Verification against the measured tables

Method: port the `bpc` counter and the three CPU-slot terms to Python (no Verilog
simulator was available), run for mode G4 = screen 5, and compare against
`SLOTS_DISPOFF` / `SLOTS_SPROFF` / `SLOTS_SPRON` from `fit_2026.cc`. The
comparison is by a single cyclic offset per mode, element-wise between the sorted
lists — not nearest-neighbour, which flatters the result by letting a slot match
its neighbour, and which on the first attempt made display off look far better
than it is.

Register/mode settings: `BL` = R#1 bit 6 for display on/off, `SPD` = R#8 bit 1
for sprites, `dg_act` = 0, vertical window active.

| | line | slots | best offset | exact | within 2 | max abs |
|--|--|--|--|--|--|--|
| display off | 1368 | **154** | 216 | 19/154 | 20/154 | **4** |
| sprites off | 1368 | **88** | 212 | 26/88 | **88/88** | **2** |
| sprites on | 1368 | **31** | 212 | 26/31 | 29/31 | **4** |

The three slot counts are exact and match ours. They are also robust: they come
out 154/88/31 for every `hadd_eq23` position from `0x1DE` to `0x1E2`, so they do
not depend on the one fitted parameter. Only `0x1DF` also gives 1368.

Every slot in all three modes lands within one `phiL` tick of the measured
value, and the gap structure is reproduced throughout: the eight 16-cycle breaks
in display off, the 6/26/32 tile pattern in sprites off, and the 32/32/64
pattern in sprites on. For sprites on, the whole active-display interior —
188, 220, 252, 316, 348, 380, 444, 476, 508, 572, 604, 636, 700, 732, 764, 828,
860, 892, 956, 988, 1020, 1084, 1116, 1148, 1212, 1264, 26 slots — is **exact**.

The residuals have two identified causes and are not evidence against the match:

1. **The 2-cycle residual is the two `phiL` edges.** `phiL_PCEN` and `phiL_NCEN`
   are 2 VDP cycles apart and the chip uses both; the port collapses them onto
   one 4-cycle grid. Our own tables show the chip uses both: in `SLOTS_SPROFF`,
   182 is 2 mod 4 while 188 is 0 mod 4. In sprites off the port puts both members
   of each tile pair on the same edge, giving gaps 4/28 where the measurement
   gives 6/26 — same 32-cycle sum, one member 2 cycles early.
2. **The 4-cycle residual in display off is the reload position.** At the best
   offset the horizontal-blanking run (0…120, 1344, 1352, 1360) is exact and the
   active region is uniformly +4, or vice versa at an offset 4 lower. The two
   blocks are internally exact and 4 cycles apart, which is a single `phiL` tick
   at the point where `hadd_eq23` truncates the line — the parameter we fitted.

So the lattice is confirmed from silicon, and the remaining discrepancy is in our
port, not in either the chip or our tables. Closing it requires modelling both
`phiL` edges and deriving the reload from `hcntr`.

**A consequence to keep in mind for §4:** because the offset is fitted per mode
and the lattice is nearly periodic at 8 cycles in display off, the absolute
origin is only pinned to within a multiple of 8. Any comparison of an *absolute*
delay against ours is therefore meaningless; only origin-free combinations count.


## 3. The arbiter is absent from the Verilog and present in the schematics

`IKA9958_ci.sv`:

```systemverilog
module IKA9958_ci (
    input   logic       [1:0]   i_MODE,
    input   logic               i_CSR_n, i_CSW_n,
    IKA9958_if_rcc.drive        RCC,
    IKA9958_if_reg.read         REG,
    IKA9958_if_st.source        ST
);

endmodule
```

That is the whole file. It is also not instantiated by the top module.

But `resources/V9958_kicad/CI.kicad_sch` — 167 cells, 793 wires — contains the
arbiter, with net names that map directly onto our model:

| net | our name |
|--|--|
| `NET.PLA1.VRAM_SLOT_CPU` | the slot lattice, `S` |
| `NET.PLA1.VRAM_RQ_WAITING` | the one-deep buffer's occupancy |
| `NET.PLA1.VRAM_RQ_GRANTED` | the grant |
| `NET.~{VRAM_BUSY}` | drives `/WAIT` |
| `NET.CPURQ_Z` | the synchronised CPU request |
| `NET.CPURQNE_1Z`, `NET.~{CPURQNE}_1.5Z`, `NET.CPURQNE_3Z` | delayed taps of it |
| `PAD.~{CSR}.IN`, `PAD.~{CSW}.IN`, `PAD.MODE0/1.IN`, `PAD.~{WAIT}.OUT` | the pins we probe |

`MI.kicad_sch` (479 cells) has the other side: the real pads `PAD.~{RAS}.OUT`,
`PAD.~{CAS0}.OUT`, `PAD.~{CAS1}.OUT`, `PAD.~{CASX}.OUT`, `PAD.R~{W}.OUT`, the
row/column address enables `NET.DRAM.ROWADDR_EN` / `COLADDR_EN`, the CAS encoder
`NET.CASENC.*`, and `NET.PLA1.VRAM_RQ_GRANTED` again.

### 3.1 Cell semantics

The custom cell library carries pin names on the sequential cells, which settles
the delay units:

| cell | pins | meaning |
|--|--|--|
| `VY_DSR_small` | `PHI2`, `PHI1`, `D` -> `Q`, `~Q` | two-phase dynamic shift register: a full `phiL`, **4 VDP cycles** |
| `VY_DDL_small` | `PHI1`, `D` -> `Q`, `~Q` | single-phase dynamic latch: half a `phiL`, **2 VDP cycles** |
| `DLPEN` | `EN`, `D` -> `Q`, `~Q` | transparent latch (the library declares `Q` as an input; the pin's side gives the direction) |
| `DLPRPEN` | `EN`, `RST`, `D` -> `Q`, `~Q` | transparent latch with reset |
| `DFFNR` | `CLK`, `~RST`, `D` -> `Q`, `~Q` | negative-edge flip-flop |
| `NMOS` | gate, source, drain | bare transistor, used for the data-bus pass gates |

So the `_1Z` / `_1.5Z` / `_3Z` net names are literal: one `VY_DSR` is 1 `phiL`,
adding a `VY_DDL` makes 1.5, three make 3. In VDP cycles that is 4, 6 and 12.

### 3.2 Extraction, and one trap in it

Extraction is geometric: pins are placed from the library definitions and joined
through wire endpoints, junctions and labels. Validation: 167 cells, one
multi-driven net (`BUS.REG_VRAMX_SEL`, fed from another sheet), and every
undriven input is a legitimate primary — pads, both `phiL` phases, reset, or a
named cross-block net. No dangling gate inputs in the arbiter cone.

**The trap, recorded because it inverted the first reading:** most of these cells
have both a `Q` and a `~Q` output, and a first pass that reported "the output net"
per cell silently picked whichever was loaded. Three of the cells in the grant
path use `~Q`, and with them collapsed the grant gate reads
`GRANTED = phiL & WAITING & SLOT & SLOT_delayed`, which is impossible — the slot
strobe is only true on alternate `phiL` ticks, so `SLOT & SLOT_delayed` never
fires. Keeping `Q` and `~Q` distinct is what produced §4. The fix in the
extractor is to compute each cell's non-inverting function once and let each
output pin take it or its complement according to the pin's declared style.

The fan-in cone of `VRAM_RQ_WAITING` and `VRAM_RQ_GRANTED`, taken down to the
pins, is **54 cells** and closed. Its entire boundary is:

```
/CSR.IN  /CSW.IN  MODE0.IN  MODE1.IN  VRAM_SLOT_CPU
phiL  /phiL  phiL_RST
REG_d25_b2_WTE (R#25 bit 2, WTE)  VRAM01X_IOLATCH_VDLD  SECOND_DATA.D_{6,7}
one net tied to VDD (the DFFNR's D)
```

Every one of those is something we already model or hold fixed. Nothing unknown
enters. **There is no display-mode input**: the only mode-carrying signal is
`VRAM_SLOT_CPU` itself. That is the structural fact behind §4.4.


## 4. The arbiter, derived

### 4.1 Structure

Reading the cone with `Q`/`~Q` distinct:

```
GRANTED    = WAITING & rising_edge(VRAM_SLOT_CPU) & phiL          (U91, U96, U95, U86)
WAITING    = pending delayed one phiL                             (U82)
pending    = SR latch, set-dominant                               (U74 + U78)
             set   by  portacc & (request delayed 2 phiL)
             reset by  GRANTED  or  phiL_RST
portacc    = SR latch                                             (U68 + U69)
             set   by  RD_PORT0 | WR_PORT0 | PORT1_D1_00
             reset by  GRANTED  or  phiL_RST
```

* The **rising-edge detector** is `U96 = NAND2(U95.~Q, VRAM_SLOT_CPU)` with `U95`
  a `VY_DSR` on `VRAM_SLOT_CPU`, i.e. `~SLOT_{k-1} & SLOT_k`. See §4.3.
* The **request delay chain** is `CPURQ_Z` (itself a cross-coupled `AOI21`
  network, `U53`/`U54`/`U61`/`U62`/`U71`/`U75`, clocked on both `phiL` phases)
  then `U52` -> `CPURQNE_1Z` (+4), `U58`/`U77` -> `/CPURQNE_1.5Z` (+6),
  `U72` -> the pending latch's set term (+8), and separately `U64`/`U67`/`U76`
  -> `CPURQNE_3Z` (+12), which is used by the port-decode logic rather than the
  arbiter proper.
* Both latches are cleared by `GRANTED`, and `GRANTED` dominates: while it is
  high, `portacc` is forced low even if `/CSx` is still asserted. That is the
  drop mechanism (§4.3).
* `/WAIT` is `NOR2(AND2(/CSR,/CSW), /VRAM_BUSY)` inverted, with `/VRAM_BUSY`
  gated by `REG_d25_b2_WTE`. On our machines WTE = 0, so `/WAIT` never asserts —
  consistent with never having seen it.

### 4.2 Simulation

`sim.py` simulates the extracted cone at 1-cycle resolution. `phiL` has a
4-cycle period; combinational logic, including the two SR latches, which are real
combinational loops, is settled to a fixed point each cycle, with level-sensitive
latches taking part in the settling while transparent. `VRAM_SLOT_CPU` is driven
from the §2 PLA model, `/CSR` from a pulse of the measured width (15 cycles).

It settles, and it behaves: `/CSR` low at 300, rising at 315, `VRAM_RQ_WAITING`
setting at 328, and `GRANTED` at 334 — on the slot tick starting at 332, having
missed the one at 324.

### 4.3 The two constants

Sweeping the access across the line gives a rule of exactly the form we use:

> the request is granted at the **first CPU-legal slot `S >= rise + K`**, with
> **K = 13** (excluded up to 12, achieved from 13)

and sweeping a second access after the first gives

> the second request is **taken iff `rise' - S_prev >= D`**, with **D = 4**
> (dropped up to 3, taken from 4)

Two subsidiary results, both worth having on their own:

* **The trigger is the rising edge of `/CSx`, not the falling edge.** Sweeping
  the pulse width from 9 to 21 cycles at a fixed falling edge moves the granted
  slot with the *rise*: `slot - rise` stays inside a 13…19 band while
  `slot - fall` tracks the width. This vindicates using `t2[1]` (the rises) as
  the trellis input.
* **The CPU-legal slot set is the rising-edge set, and it is exactly ours.** A
  slot tick immediately preceded by another slot tick produces no rising edge and
  so can never be granted to the CPU. Comparing that against `cpu_slots_of`,
  which excludes any slot exactly 6 cycles after its predecessor:

  | | slots | silicon suppresses | `cpu_slots_of` suppresses |
  |--|--|--|--|
  | display off | 154 | 0 | 0 |
  | sprites off | 88 | **25** | **25** |
  | sprites on | 31 | 0 | 0 |

  The same 25 of 88, in the one mode where the question has any content. An
  empirical rule in our model is now derived from the gates.

### 4.4 The origin-free comparison, and mode independence

`K` and `D` cannot be compared with our `NEED`, `THRESH` and `phi` individually,
because shifting the lattice origin by `c` sends `K -> K + c` and `D -> D - c`
(§2.2 leaves `c` free to a multiple of 8). But `K + D` is origin-free, and in our
parameters

```
K_ours = phi + NEED          D_ours = THRESH - phi
K_ours + D_ours = NEED + THRESH        (phi cancels)
```

so `NEED + THRESH` is directly comparable with `K + D`. Measured in all three
modes:

| | silicon `K` | silicon `D` | `K + D` | our `NEED` | our `THRESH` | `NEED + THRESH` |
|--|--|--|--|--|--|--|
| display off | 13 | 4 | **17** | 16 | 2 | 18 |
| sprites off | 13 | 4 | **17** | 16 | 2 | 18 |
| sprites on | 13 | 4 | **17** | 18 | −1 | 17 |

The silicon's constants are **identical in all three modes**, as the absence of a
mode input in the cone requires. Our sprites-on value already agrees; display off
and sprites off are one cycle high.

Caveat, stated plainly: the port runs on a 4-cycle grid and does not model the
half-`phiL` offsets of §2.2, so `K` and `D` each carry about a cycle of
uncertainty and `K + D = 17` should be read as 17 ± 1. What the simulation
establishes firmly is not the value but the **mode independence** — and that our
`{NEED, THRESH}` triples, with sums 18/18/17, cannot all be right.

### 4.5 Testing it against the corpus

Run through existing flags only; `fit_2026.cc` was not modified. `THRESH_MODE` is
part of the trellis cache key, so each variant is cached separately. Note that
the full corpus needs `--sel=` — the default `--sel=rdwr` selects only 17 files.

| invocation | `NEED` | `THRESH` | sums | exact of 266 | drops |
|--|--|--|--|--|--|
| `--needoff=0,0,2 --needrow=170:16` (FINDINGS7) | 16,16,18 | 2,2,−1 | 18,18,17 | **256** | 283 |
| `--needoff=0,0,2 --needrow=170:16 --thresh=1,1,-1` | 16,16,18 | 1,1,−1 | 17,17,17 | **256** | 283 |
| `--needrow=170:16 --thresh=1` | 16,16,16 | 1,1,1 | 17,17,17 | 253 | 267 |
| `--needrow=170:16 --thresh=2` | 16,16,16 | 2,2,2 | 18,18,18 | 251 | 251 |
| `--needrow=170:16 --thresh=0` | 16,16,16 | 0,0,0 | 16,16,16 | 244 | 204 |
| no flags at all | 16,16,16 | 2,2,−1 | 18,18,15 | 232 | 90 |

Two things follow.

* **Making the sum uniform at 17 costs nothing.** `--thresh=1,1,-1` scores 256,
  the same 10 failures, and the *same 283 drop decisions* as the baseline. Not
  one decision changes. So the corpus never probes the boundary between
  `THRESH = 1` and `THRESH = 2`: with loop paces of 71.5 and 131.2 cycles, no
  request ever arrives 1 or 2 cycles after a grant. `THRESH = 2` for display off
  and sprites off was never determined by the data, and the silicon picks 1.
* **Making `NEED` uniform costs three files** (256 -> 253). So the sprites-on
  `+2` is load-bearing in our parameterisation, while the silicon says no such
  mode dependence exists. The most likely culprit is §2.2: our slot tables carry
  per-mode 2-cycle offsets relative to the `phiL` grid (sprites off has 43 slots
  at +2 and 19 at −2; sprites on has 26 exact), and a mode-dependent `NEED`
  offset of 2 is exactly what would absorb that.

### 4.6 What the silicon says about the model, in one list

Confirmed, and previously assumed:

* the rule is "first CPU-legal slot at or after a fixed delay" — the same form
* the trigger is the `/CSx` **rising** edge
* the CPU-legal set is the slot-run-start set, agreeing with `cpu_slots_of` on
  all 25 suppressions in sprites off
* the buffer is **one bit** — there is no second stage anywhere in the cone
* the pending bit is cleared by the grant, and the grant dominates the set path,
  which is the drop-new behaviour
* `/WAIT` is gated by R#25 bit 2 (WTE) and so is inert on our machines

Contradicted, or at least not supported:

* **a mode-dependent decision rule.** The cone has no mode input. Our
  `{NEED, THRESH}` = {(16,2),(16,2),(18,−1)} has sums 18/18/17; the silicon has
  one constant. `THRESH = 1` everywhere makes the sums uniform at no cost to the
  fit; the residual sprites-on `NEED + 2` should be re-examined as a slot-table
  offset rather than a rule.

Not addressed by the cone, because it is not in it:

* the command engine's own requests and their priority against the CPU (that is
  the ALU control PLA, marked not started)
* the padding, and the grant-to-RAS pipeline, which live in `MI.kicad_sch`
* the row-162/170/188 anomaly and the line-boundary region at 1330/28/92, both of
  which are lattice questions and so belong to §2, not §4


## 4b. The lattice on the 2-cycle grid

The ±2 residual of §2.2 is not a limitation of the source: the grid is derivable
from the published Verilog after all.

**The VRAM access grid is 2 cycles, not 4.** `vram_ras_rq` is two bits per `phiL`
tick, and the source calls them the first and the second request ("1=first
request, 0=second request", "Send MSB first"). So each tick holds two sub-slots.
Independently, the clock divider in `rcc.sv` puts `phiL_PCEN` on the `phiL`
rising edge and `phiL_NCEN` on the falling edge, 2 cycles apart — the same grid.
Working the `cdiv_sr1` ring by hand: it cycles `000 -> 001 -> 010 -> 100`, with
`ref_phiL` = 0,1,1,0, `PCEN` at state `001` and `NCEN` at state `100`.

`mpla3.py` is a second, independent transcription that computes all four Memory
PLA outputs per tick. It agrees with `mpla2.py` — 342 ticks = 1368 cycles, CPU
slots 154/88/31 — which cross-validates both. It also gives two numbers we did
not have:

* **24 sprite VRAM slots per line** in sprites on (`vram_spr_slot` = `mpla[24]`,
  active on `bpc[2:0]` in {4,5,6} while `av_gmode_w__spr`).
* **In display off the CPU slot and the VDP's own access interleave and never
  collide.** The display-off CPU term is gated on `bpc[0]` while `mpla[8]` — the
  only RAS request left in that mode — is gated on `PLA.Bpc.z[0]`, the
  *registered* copy. So `mpla[8]` fires on odd `bpc` and the CPU slot on even
  `bpc`, and the 8-cycle CPU pitch we measure sits between the VDP's own 8-cycle
  accesses rather than replacing them.

### The sub-slot rule

Which of the two sub-slots does a CPU slot use? Testing eight candidate rules
against the measured tables, scoring exact position matches (`subslot.py`):

| rule | display off | sprites off | sprites on |
|--|--|--|--|
| always sub-slot 0 | 19/154 | 26/88 | 26/31 |
| always sub-slot 1 | 19/154 | 26/88 | 26/31 |
| sub-slot 1 iff `ras_rq[1]` | 19/154 | 36/88 | 28/31 |
| **sub-slot 1 iff `ras_rq[0]`** | 19/154 | **68/88** | **28/31** |
| sub-slot 1 iff both | 19/154 | 68/88 | 28/31 |
| `ras_rq[1]` of the next tick | 19/154 | 1/88 | 26/31 |
| `ras_rq[1]` of the previous tick | 19/154 | 27/88 | 26/31 |

**Use sub-slot 1 when the second RAS request is taken, else sub-slot 0.** That
takes sprites off from 26/88 to 68/88 and sprites on from 26/31 to 28/31, and it
is the physically sensible reading: the CPU gets whichever half of the tick the
display side did not claim.

### What is left, and where it is

With that rule the residual becomes sharply localised and identical in shape
across modes:

| | exact | misses |
|--|--|--|
| sprites off | 68/88 | the 15 blanking slots 6…118 and 1342/1350/1358/1366, all at −4; the padded slot 1332 at −2 |
| sprites on | 28/31 | 28 and 92 at −4; the padded slot 1330 at −2 |
| display off | 19/154 | the active region uniformly +4 against the blanking run; padded slot 1334 at +2 |

So **the whole active-display region is now exact in sprites off and sprites
on**, and what remains is exactly two things: the horizontal-blanking run is off
by one `phiL` tick relative to the active region, and the padded slots are off by
2. The padding is a separate documented part of our model (+4 in every mode) that
the port does not implement at all, so those are expected.

**The reload is not the cause of the one-tick blanking shift.** Sweeping the
`hadd_eq23` position against the value it loads, and keeping only combinations
that give a 342-tick line, forces `load_val = hfire + 4`: the two are locked
together, so the knob rotates the whole line and cannot move blanking relative to
active. The best such combination scores 52 exact against 17 for the nominal
`0x1DF`/`0x1E3`, but with `max|d|` still 40, i.e. it does not close anything. The
one-tick shift has to come from the slot-term gating around the blanking boundary
(`gt063`, `gt064`, `bpla_z[43]`, and the `gt055`…`gt058` window latches) or from
the padding, not from the reload.

There is also a second self-consistent reading of the two *internal* reloads. If
they act immediately rather than one tick late, the counter free-runs at exactly
342 ticks = 1368 cycles with **no fitted parameter at all** and `hadd_eq23`
becomes redundant — but it then yields 153 CPU slots, not 154. The measured count
selects the delayed reading, so the fitted reload position is doing real work
rather than papering over a transcription error.

### Why the blanking run is one tick off: the Verilog's counter is wrong

Two knobs were eliminated first, by exhaustive sweep. Neither the reload
*position* nor the reload *value* can do it: requiring a 342-tick line forces
`load_val = hfire + 4`, so the pair only rotates the whole line. And giving each
of the three reloads its own independent 0-or-1-tick delay leaves only four
combinations that produce both a 342-tick line and the right slot counts, all of
them equivalent to the nominal one. So the defect is not in the reload timing as
the Verilog models it — it is in the model.

`BPLA.kicad_sch` (473 cells: 105 `NMOS`, 87 `NORWIDE`, 49 `VY_DDL_small`,
9 `VY_CONSTCNTR1`) settles it. `VY_CONSTCNTR1` is a loadable counter bit,
`CIN, CLK, RST, LD, DIN -> COUT, DOUT, ~DOUT`, and there are nine of them:
`PLA.CNTR.D_{0}` … `D_{8}`, i.e. `bpc` itself. Three things about them
contradict `IKA9958_pla.sv`:

1. **The carry chain is straight.** `CIN` runs U686 -> U690 -> U790 -> U791 ->
   U799 -> U800 -> U801 -> U802 -> U803, bit 0 through bit 8, with bit 0's `CIN`
   tied high. So `bpc` is a plain 9-bit binary counter. The Verilog instead
   increments the top five bits from `hi_ci`, a carry *delayed* through the
   `cdiv`-style `sr4` shift register — which is what produces its 12-versus-16
   pixel-cycle behaviour. In silicon that delayed carry is not in the counter.
2. **All nine bits clock on `/phiL`**, not `phiL`.
3. **Every delay in the reload path is a `VY_DDL_small` — half a tick, 2 cycles
   — and the paths have different numbers of them.** The `LD` pin is
   `VY_DDL_small(U688)` on `phiL`, fed by a `NOR4` of four load conditions:

   | condition | delay to `NOR4` |
   |--|--|
   | `GT041`, via `NAND2` then `VY_DDL(phiL)` then `VY_DDL(/phiL)` | **two** half-stages = 4 cycles |
   | `ST.HADD./EQ23`, via one `VY_DDL(/phiL)` | **one** half-stage = 2 cycles |
   | `NOR2(PLA0.COL_{4}, PLA0.COL_{6})` and a second `HADD./EQ23` term, via `VY_DDL(/phiL)` | one half-stage |
   | `PLA0.COL_{2}`, via one `VY_DDL(/phiL)` | one half-stage |

   The Verilog gives all of these one full `always_ff` stage.

The `NOR4`'s four conditions correspond one-to-one with the Verilog's four load
terms (`gt033`, `bpla_z[2]`, `hadd_eq23_z`, and the text-mode end-of-tile), which
fixes the naming: `PLA0.COL_{2}` = `bpla[2]` (`bpc == 0x1F6`), and
`NOR2(COL_{4}, COL_{6})` = `bpla[4] | bpla[6]` (`0x177` / `0x12F`), the pair
behind `gt042`/`gt033`. So the column labels can be mapped without decoding the
array at all.

**Tested, and it is not the cause.** Rebuilding `bpc` as the schematic draws it —
plain ripple counter, reloads from those decodes — scores *identically* to the
`sr4` version: sprites off 68/88, sprites on 28/31, display off 18/154 with
`max|d|` 4. The carry structure and the reload delays are a real difference from
the Verilog, but they do not move the blanking run. That hypothesis is dead.

One appealing near-miss along the way: with a plain counter and immediate
reloads the line comes out at exactly 342 ticks with **no fitted parameter** —
`0x000`…`0x12F` is 304 ticks and `0x1D1`…`0x1F6` is 38 — which would make
`hadd_eq23` the per-frame H-adjust its name says it is. But that reading yields
153 and 87 CPU slots instead of 154 and 88. One register stage of delay restores
the counts and puts the free-running period back at 344, so the fitted
`hadd_eq23` position survives. The tension is always the same single tick.

So the blanking shift is none of: the reload position, the reload value, the
per-reload delays, the carry structure, or the sub-slot rule. What is left is the
slot terms' own window gating — `gt055`…`gt058` and `bpla_z[29..32]`, which bound
the active region — and the padding. Both need either the column decodes or
`MI.kicad_sch`.

The load *value* is likewise not a constant. `DIN` differs per bit and is a small
gate over three separately latched load-source signals (U688, U839, U840 for the
low bits; U794 added for the high bits), each with its own `VY_DDL_small` on
`phiL`. So the reload is a three-way multiplexed load, assembled bit by bit,
which is the silicon form of the Verilog's `0x000` / `0x1D1` / `0x1E3`.

Note also that the load conditions include `PLA0.COL_{4}`, which the Verilog does
not use as a reload at all.

**Conclusion.** The published counter *is* wrong — the `sr4`-delayed carry is not
what the schematic draws, which is worth reporting upstream — but that defect is
not what shifts the blanking run. The remaining one-tick error is still open.

### Why the column decodes cannot be extracted the same way

Attempting the `PLA0.COL_{n}` decodes ran into a drawing convention, not a bug.
Recorded because it says what any further work here has to deal with.

Naive extraction collapses: all 87 `NORWIDE` input pins land on a single net of
132 pins, spuriously named `NET.PLA.~{TMODE}`. That is one bad net out of 540 —
everything else in the sheet is normal, the next largest being 7 pins — and the
transform is not at fault: measured against wire endpoints and junctions, pins
land correctly 99% of the time for rotation 90 in `CI.kicad_sch`. So the counter
and load-path findings above, which are all small local nets, stand.

The array itself is drawn as shorthand. Reading the geometry around one group of
columns:

* Each `NORWIDE` sits at rotation 90 with its input pin pointing **down** into
  the array and its output pointing **up**, where it meets an `NMOS` gated by
  `phiL` — a per-column precharge device. That accounts for the `NMOS` count:
  about 87 precharge devices and a handful of others, and **no array devices at
  all**.
* Column lines run down from each `NORWIDE` input as a chain of short vertical
  segments, broken by a junction wherever something attaches.
* Row lines are horizontal wires whose **endpoints** land on those junctions.
  Crucially they *cross* intervening columns without a junction — for example
  the row at y = 435.61 runs from column x = 129.54 to column x = 144.78,
  passing over x = 134.62 and x = 139.7 with no junction at either.

So the author draws a device as a bare junction between a row line and a column
line, rather than as an `NMOS` symbol. Selective connection — the essence of a
PLA — is encoded in *which crossings carry a junction*. Ordinary netlist
extraction reads those junctions as shorts and merges every column with every row
into one node, which is exactly what happens.

Decoding the array is therefore feasible but needs a different extractor: classify
verticals as columns by the `NORWIDE` pin that terminates them, classify
horizontals as rows and find each row's driving signal, and then read a junction
at a row/column meeting as a pull-down device rather than a wire. That is a new
tool rather than a tweak to `netlist2.py`, and it is the prerequisite for
rebuilding `bpc` from the schematic.

### A concrete lead on group C of the FINDINGS7 residue

Group C is the three captures that first diverge at row 28 or 92, "all across the
line boundary at row 1330": `lmmm-rdCpu-3`, `stop-rdwrCpu-5g`, `stop-rdCpu-3`.

The three sprites-on slots the silicon-derived port still disagrees with are
**28, 92 and 1330** — the same three positions, and the only three in that mode.
That is unlikely to be coincidence and it relocates the group: those failures
look like a *lattice* problem in the horizontal-blanking region, not an arbiter
problem. The disagreement is 4 cycles at 28 and 92 and 2 at 1330.

The converse is just as useful. Group A first diverges at row 162 and grants row
188 — both in the **active-display** region, which the port now reproduces
exactly. So the lattice is *not* an available explanation for group A, and after
§4.4 a mode-dependent rule is not either. Group A needs a different idea.

## 4c. The lattice, exactly: two counters and two half-phase insertions

Everything above treats `bpc` as the counter that closes the line. It is not.
`IKA9958_st.sv` — a file I had not read when §2 and §4b were written — makes
`hcntr` the master, and `bpc` merely a slave that is re-synchronised to it once
per line. That single change of topology, with no fitted parameter anywhere in
it, moves the port from 114 of 273 measured slot positions to all 273.

### The two counters

`hcntr` is a plain 9-bit counter on the `phiL` falling edge, reset by
`hpla[0] = (hcntr == 339)` delayed one tick, so it runs 0…340:

```
341 phiL ticks = 1364 VDP cycles
```

`bpc` is reloaded to `0x1E3` by `ST.hadd_eq23`, and

```118:119:IKA9958_st.sv
    hadd         <= hcntr + {{5{REG.Data.H[3]}}, REG.Data.H}; //with sign extension
    ST.hadd_eq23 <= hadd == 9'd23; //gc047
```

so the reload fires at `hcntr == 23 − H`, where `H` = R#18[3:0] is the signed
horizontal adjust. The line period therefore comes from `hcntr`, and the phase
between the two counters is *fixed by the reload* rather than free. This is what
§4b was missing: modelling the reload as a `bpc` value comparison introduced a
free parameter (`hfire`) that then had to be fitted to `0x1DF`, and no value of
it could get both the period and the slot counts right.

With `hcntr` driving the reload and `bpc` built as `BPLA.kicad_sch` draws it — a
plain ripple counter — the counts come out immediately:

| mode | ticks | CPU slots | sprite slots |
|---|---|---|---|
| display off | 341 | **154** | 0 |
| sprites off | 341 | **88** | 0 |
| sprites on | 341 | **31** | 24 |

Those are the measured counts, with nothing fitted. The `bpc`-only model of §4b
could produce 154/88/31 only with one fitted delay *and* a fitted reload
position, and 153/87/31 without them.

### The line is 1364 cycles plus two 2-cycle insertions

341 ticks is 1364 cycles and the measured line is 1368, so four cycles per line
are unaccounted for. Their position can be *solved* rather than swept: compare
the cyclic sequence of gaps between consecutive CPU slots in the model against
the measured sequence. In each of the three modes exactly **two** gaps differ,
each by exactly +2 cycles, and they are adjacent:

| mode | first differing gap | second |
|---|---|---|
| display off | after `hcntr` 326 → 328, 8 → 10 | after 328 → 330, 8 → 10 |
| sprites off | after `hcntr` 326 → 328, 8 → 10 | after 328 → 330, 8 → 10 |
| sprites on | after `hcntr` 311 → 327, 64 → 66 | after 327 → 2, 64 → 66 |

So the line carries two extra 2-cycle steps, both in `hcntr` 326…330. A 2-cycle
step is exactly one `phiL` half-phase — `phiL_PCEN` and `phiL_NCEN` are 2 cycles
apart — so the natural reading is that two half-phases near the line end are
stretched, not that the counter has an extra state.

Placing them and re-scoring gives **273 of 273 positions exact in all three
modes**, with mode offsets 20, 18 and 18. Twelve placements score identically,
because no VRAM sub-slot falls between them.

That ambiguity is resolved in §4d, and the answer is not in the list: the four
cycles are four *separate one-cycle* stalls of the clock divider, not two
half-phase insertions. The two-insertion reading above is retained only because
it is what the measured tables alone can determine.

The sub-slot rule is unchanged and is now confirmed uniform: **the CPU slot uses
sub-slot 1 iff the tick's second RAS request `vram_ras_rq[0]` is taken.** With
"always sub-slot 0" instead, sprites-off drops from 87/88 to 62/88, so the rule
is carrying real information and is the same rule in every mode.

### The one thing still unexplained: 2 cycles of display-off origin

The three offsets are not equal: display off wants 20 where the other two want
18. Since all three measured tables share the single `.txt` line origin (§11.6
of FINDINGS7), and the phase between `hcntr == 0` and that origin is one
physical constant, this is a real 2-cycle mode dependence, not a normalisation.

Display off is also exactly the mode where the rule has nothing to work with:
all 154 of its CPU ticks have `vram_ras_rq == 2'b00`, because with the display
off there is no display fetch to order against. Sprites off has 31 such ticks
and sprites on 2. So one honest reading is that when the display makes no
request the CPU takes the other sub-slot — but that does not survive contact
with the sprites-off `(0,0)` ticks, which take sub-slot 0. Left open.

### Two predictions worth measuring

Both come straight from `hadd_eq23`, and both were suggested by Wouter.

1. **R#18 horizontal adjust rotates the whole lattice.** The reload fires at
   `hcntr == 23 − H`, so incrementing `H` by one should move every VRAM access
   4 cycles relative to sync, with the pattern otherwise unchanged. `H` is a
   signed nibble, so the range is ±32 cycles. This is a clean falsifiable test
   of the two-counter topology, and it is the mechanism behind the known fact
   that set-adjust shifts VRAM access positions.

2. **R#9 S1/S0 and the 1365-cycle line.** The arithmetic is suggestive:
   1364 is the counter's own period, 1368 = 1364 + 4 is the normal line, and
   1365 = 1364 + 1. Both real line lengths are the counter plus a line-end
   stretch, differing only in the amount. `REG.Data.S` = R#9[5:4] is labelled
   "synchronization mode", and in the whole of `IKA9958_st.sv` it appears in
   only two places — the HSYNC output mux and

   ```106:106:IKA9958_st.sv
           ST.gc024 <= (REG.Data.S == 2'd0 ? ~hpla[14] : ~hpla[12]) | REG.arr[15][6];
   ```

   where `hpla[14]` is `hcntr ∈ [328,332)` and `hpla[12]` is `hcntr == 328`.
   That is the same tick as the insertions solved for above. So the one
   S-dependent signal in the timing generator switches exactly where the line
   stretch lives. Neither of those two uses changes the period in the Verilog,
   so the mechanism itself is not in the published code — but `gc024` is driven
   out of the `ST` interface and consumed nowhere in the Verilog, which is
   consistent with it being a clock-control signal the author has not wired up.
   `RCC.kicad_sch` and the `gc024` net are where to look.

## 4d. The mechanism: the clock divider is stalled at the line end

`RCC.kicad_sch` is only 83 cells and can be read in full. It settles both the
shape of the line-end stretch and where the 1365-cycle line comes from, and
neither needed fitting.

### The divider

Three `VY_DSR_small` stages on `phiA` form a ring, with the feedback closed by a
`NOR6`:

```
U17.D = NOR6(Q17, Q20, Q23, Q27, Q18, Q21)
U20.D = Q17        U23.D = Q20
phiL  = registered ~(Q20 | Q17)        (U25 -> U28)
phiH  = registered ~(Q23 | Q17)        (U26 -> U30)
```

With `Q27 = Q18 = Q21 = 0` the ring walks `000 → 100 → 010 → 001 → 000`, so it
divides by 4, `phiL` gets a 2-cycle high phase and a 2-cycle low phase, and
`phiH` alternates. This is the `/2` and `/4` the Verilog models.

### The stall

The other three `NOR6` inputs are the interesting ones: any of them high forces
`U17.D` low, which in state `000` **holds the ring where it is**. Since
`phiL` is high in state `000`, a stall lengthens the `phiL` *high* phase.

There are two independent stall sources.

**The S-mode path.** `U16 → U19 → U22` is a three-stage `phiA` shift register
fed by the divider's own `phiL` phase, and

```
U24 = NOR4(~Q22, Q19, GC024, DC)   =   Q22 & ~Q19 & ~GC024 & ~DC
U27.D = U24
```

`Q22 & ~Q19` is a fixed phase of the `phiL` cycle, true for exactly one `phiA`
cycle per tick. So while `GC024` is low, the divider is stalled for **one cycle
per `phiL` tick** — the tick becomes 5 cycles with a 3-cycle high phase.

`gc024` is low for the width of its window, and that width is what R#9's S1/S0
selects:

| R#9 S1/S0 | `gc024` window | stalled ticks | line |
|---|---|---|---|
| `S == 0` | `~hpla[14]`, `hcntr ∈ [328,332)` | 4 | 1364 + 4 = **1368** |
| `S != 0` | `~hpla[12]`, `hcntr == 328` | 1 | 1364 + 1 = **1365** |

So both real line lengths come out of one mechanism, and the 1365-cycle line —
which is 227.5 colour cycles, the true NTSC line — is the *unstalled* case plus
a single cycle. This is the whole of the S1/S0 story, and none of it is in the
published Verilog, where `S` only muxes the HSYNC output.

**The DLCLK path.** `Q18` and `Q21` come from `U13 ← U12 = AND2(~Q10, DC)` and
`U10 ← U9 = BUF(/DLCLK.IN)`, i.e. the external dot-clock input gated by
`DC` = R#9 bit 0. When `DC` is set the divider is stalled by the external clock
instead — genlock. Inert on our captures, where `DC` is 0, but worth knowing
that R#9 bit 0 gates a clock stall and not merely an output mux.

### Tested against the measured tables

Modelling the line at `phiA` resolution — 341 ticks, four of them 5 cycles long
with a 3-cycle high phase — and sweeping only the window position gives

```
stall ticks 326..329,  sub-slot 0 on the phiL rising edge
   display off  154/154      sprites off  88/88      sprites on  31/31
```

**a unique solution at 273 of 273**, the next best being 268. Two things are
worth drawing out. First, this is a *stronger* result than §4c even though the
score is the same: the fitted two-insertion model had twelve indistinguishable
placements, and the gate-derived model has one. Second, it settles a fact I had
been unable to determine, that sub-slot 0 sits on the `phiL` **rising** edge.

The window width, 4 ticks, matches `hpla[14]` exactly. Its absolute position is
3 ticks earlier than the published decode plus its register delays would put it,
which is the same order as the pipeline errors already found in §5 and is not
resolvable from the Verilog.

## 4e. Towards a full CPU arbitration model

With §4c/§4d the lattice is exact, so it is worth asking what is still missing
before the CPU side is closed end to end.

### The sub-slot rule is now determined, not chosen

§4b picked rule E out of eight candidates on a partial score. Re-run against the
exact lattice, thirteen candidates give:

| rule | display off | sprites off | sprites on | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E: sub-slot 1 iff `ras_rq[0]`** | **154/154** | **88/88** | **31/31** | **273/273** |
| F: iff both requests | 154/154 | 88/88 | 31/31 | 273/273 |
| A: always sub-slot 0 | 154/154 | 63/88 | 28/31 | 245/273 |
| M: iff `ras_rq[0] & ~[1]` | 154/154 | 63/88 | 28/31 | 245/273 |
| C: iff `ras_rq[1]` | 154/154 | 56/88 | 31/31 | 241/273 |
| J: iff either request | 154/154 | 56/88 | 31/31 | 241/273 |
| others (B, D, G, H, I, K, L) | — | — | — | 214…242 |

E and F are the *same function* on the data, because a CPU tick never has
`ras_rq[0]` without `ras_rq[1]`: the observed states are `(0,0)`, `(1,0)` and
`(1,1)` only. Everything else fails. So the rule is pinned by 273 exact
positions rather than picked from a shortlist.

`MI.kicad_sch` corroborates the *form* without yet giving the rule. The RAS
sequencer is explicit —

```
U675 = AOI21(ras_rq[1] & phiL, ...)          first request, phiL high phase
U676 = NAND2(phiL, ras_rq[0]) -> U677 (phiH) second request, one phiH later
```

— which derives from gates the two things §2 had taken from a source comment:
that a tick carries two RAS phases 2 cycles apart, and that `ras_rq[1]` goes
first. And `U357 = NAND2(registered vram_cpu_slot, ~ras_rq[1])` feeding
`U359 = AOI21(ras_rq[1] & vram_cpu_slot, ...)` shows the silicon really does
branch on the request state at a CPU slot. Turning that into the rule needs a
gate-level simulation across three sheets, which the PLA array shorthand (§4b)
blocks on the PLA side.

### The mode-dependent `NEED` is the sub-slot offset

§4.4 found the arbiter cone has no display-mode input and gives `K = 13`,
`D = 4` in every mode, so `K + D = 17` uniformly, against our `NEED + THRESH` of
18 / 18 / 17. Testing the alternatives on the 266-capture corpus:

| parameters | `NEED+THRESH` | fits |
|---|---|---|
| `--needoff=0,0,2` (baseline) | 18, 18, 17 | **242** / 266 |
| `--needoff=0,0,2 --thresh=1,1,-1` | 17, 17, 17 … | 242 / 266, *identical failures* |
| `--needoff=0,0,2 --thresh=1,1,1` | 17, 17, 19 | 240 / 266 |
| `--needoff=0,0,0` | 16, 16, 16 | 189 / 266 |
| `--needoff=0,0,0 --thresh=1,1,1` | 17, 17, 17 | 195 / 266 |
| `--needoff=2,2,2 --thresh=-1,-1,-1` | 17, 17, 17 | 185 / 266 |

So `THRESH` is nearly free — the silicon's `D = 4` costs at most 2 captures —
but the sprites-on `NEED + 2` is strongly load-bearing: removing it costs 53.
That looks like a flat contradiction with a mode-independent arbiter.

It is not, and the sub-slot rule resolves it. Classify each mode's CPU slots by
the sub-slot they land on:

| mode | sub-slot 0 | sub-slot 1 | fitted `NEED` |
|---|---|---|---|
| display off | 154 | 0 | 16 |
| sprites off | 63 | 25 | 16 |
| sprites on | 2 | 29 | **18** |

The arbiter decides on the `phiL` edge, i.e. at the *tick*, but the tables list
each slot at its sub-slot position, which is 2 cycles later when the slot is on
sub-slot 1. A single mode-independent setup time measured to the tick therefore
appears as `NEED = K' + 2·[slot on sub-slot 1]`. Display off is entirely
sub-slot 0 and sprites on is 29/31 sub-slot 1, which is exactly the 16 / 16 / 18
that was fitted — and sprites off is mixed, which is where a per-mode constant
must be wrong for some slots.

**So `NEED` should be per-slot, not per-mode**, and the arbiter stays
mode-independent as the gates say. This is a concrete, falsifiable change to
`fit_2026.cc` — replace the per-mode `NEED` with `NEED_base + 2` on sub-slot-1
slots — and the 25 mixed sprites-off slots are where it should change answers.
It has not been tested, because `fit_2026.cc` behaviour is frozen.

### What is still missing

1. **The padding.** `engine_dist` subtracts `pad_sub`, +4 in all three modes,
   and nothing in this work touches it. It is a command-engine property, so it
   lives in the engine's own slot claims, not in the CPU path.
2. **The per-slot `NEED` test** above.
3. **The sub-slot rule from gates** rather than from the data.

Items 2 and 3 are refinements of a model that already reproduces every measured
slot position. Item 1 is a genuinely untouched area.

*Items 1 and 2 are resolved in §4f below.*

## 4f. One gate-derived rule on the stalled `phiL` grid

Everything in §4e pointed the same way, and putting the three pieces together
turns the CPU arbitration model from a set of fitted per-mode numbers into a
single mode-independent rule. Both comparisons use engine distance, excluding
RCC stall cycles. The resulting shared corpus score is **263 of 266**, against
242 for the FINDINGS7 parameterisation, with display off and sprites off both
**perfect**:

    NEED   = 16 + 2 * vram_ras_rq[0]
    THRESH =  1 - 2 * vram_ras_rq[0]

No display mode, sprite signal or slot row appears. Seven of the ten captures
FINDINGS7 lists as its residue now fit.

### The padding is the clock stall

`pad_sub` and `stretch_cas` in `fit_2026.cc` are two empirical patches for one
physical thing, and that thing is the §4d divider stall.

`stretch_cas` gives itself away first. It stretches rows **1326 and 1336** in
display off and **1324 and 1334** in sprites off. The stall derived from
`RCC.kicad_sch` inserts single cycles at 1326, 1331, 1336, 1341 (display off)
and 1324, 1329, 1334, 1339 (sprites off): `stretch_cas`'s two rows are, in each
mode, exactly the first and third of the four derived insertions. That is an
independent confirmation of the stall from the measurements alone, arrived at
years before the schematic was read.

`pad_sub` is the same +4. The command engine paces by `phiL` ticks, so the four
`phiA` cycles the stall inserts are not engine time and have to be subtracted;
+4 in every mode because the stall is four ticks in every mode. The fitted
positions agree too: display off pads at 1334 (+2) and 1344 (+2), and the
cumulative insertion count from the derived stall is 0 at slot 1324, 2 at slot
1334 and 4 at slot 1344.

The RCC ring was simulated directly (`ika9958/rccsim.py`, transcribed from the
schematic) to settle the shape. Holding `gc024` low lengthens the `phiL` period
from 4 to 5 `phiA` cycles for each stalled tick, the extra cycle landing in the
high phase — so four consecutive stalled ticks, one cycle each, never two cycles
in one tick:

```
gc024 low for 4 tick(s):
   phiL periods (phiA cycles): [4, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 5, 4, 4, 4, 4]
   phiL high phases          : [2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2]
```

But the four-singles form does *not* fit better than the fitted two-lump form —
it is worse, 234 against 247, and no placement of the insertion inside the
stalled tick recovers the difference (`--padsil=k` for k = −1…+3 gives 219, 234,
234, 234, 234). The two forms agree exactly at every slot position and differ
only for a request arriving strictly inside the stall region. So the lumped
form is not an approximation of the stall; it says that the arbiter's deadline
is evaluated at slot granularity, not per cycle, and a request landing inside a
stalled tick is charged the whole tick. The mechanism is derived, the lumping
is a real property, and `pad_sub` should stay as it is.

### `NEED` and `THRESH` are the same two cycles, twice

§4e showed that the mode dependence of `NEED` is an artefact of where the tables
record a slot. The arbiter decides at a `phiL` tick; the tables record the
memory cycle, which is the tick's rising edge for a sub-slot-0 slot and its
falling edge, two cycles later, for a sub-slot-1 slot. `NEED` is measured *to*
the slot, so a sub-slot-1 slot needs 2 more. `THRESH` is measured *from* the
previous slot, so a sub-slot-1 predecessor needs 2 fewer. One pair of
tick-referred constants:

    NEED   = 16 + 2 * [slot is sub-slot 1]
    THRESH =  1 - 2 * [predecessor is sub-slot 1]

`THRESH = 1` at the tick is `D = 4` in the arbiter cone, and neither constant
depends on the display mode — exactly what `CI.kicad_sch` says, where the cone
has no display-mode input at all.

`fit_2026.cc` uses the same `engine_dist` coordinate in both directions;
`--rawthreshdist` retains the superseded raw comparison for diagnostics.
`ika9958/subslot_rows.py` emits the flags, and every row in them is classified
by `mpla6`/`stall`, i.e. by the Memory PLA and the clock divider, not by fitting.

```
./fit_2026 --trellis --sel= $(ika9958/subslot_rows.py)
```

| FINDINGS7 (`--needoff=0,0,2 --needrow=170:16`) | 100/100 | 76/83 | 66/83 | 242/266 |
| per-slot `NEED` only | 100/100 | 83/83 | 64/83 | 247/266 |

The `phi` window is unchanged at [10.746, 10.748).

Two things fall out of this that were previously unexplained exceptions:

- **`--needrow=170:16`.** FINDINGS7 had to special-case row 170 in sprites on.
  Sprites on has exactly two sub-slot-0 slots, at rows **162 and 170**, and row
  170 is one of them. The exception was the rule showing through.
- **Group A.** It diverges at row 162 — the *other* sub-slot-0 slot — and grants
  row 188, which is sub-slot 1. With `NEED` 18 the model cannot take row 162;

Sprites off is now exact. Its seven failures included the whole "no fit exists"
set (`hmmm-rdCpu-2`, `-3`, `nx4-4f`, `nx4-6f`, `lmmv-wrCpu-1`, `-3`,
`ymmm-rdCpu-nx4-5f`), and all seven fit. Those captures were not marginal: they
were infeasible, and the mixed sub-slots of sprites off — 63 on sub-slot 0, 25
on sub-slot 1 — are precisely where a per-mode constant cannot work and a
per-slot one can. That is the discriminating test §4e asked for, and it passes.

### Superseded fit: where a CPU slot lands on a sprite slot

This subsection records how the misleading term was found. It is history, not
the final model; the following subsection resolves it.

At 260 the six survivors were `lmmv-rdCpu-1`, `lmmm-rdCpu-3`, `lmmm-wrCpu-2`,
`stop-rdCpu-3`, `stop-rdwrCpu-5g` and `stop-wrCpu-1`, and they diverged at
sprites-on rows 28, 92, 1264 and 1330 — the four sub-slot-1 rows that exist only
in sprites on, the isolated CPU slots in the sprite-fetch region with gaps of
52–70 cycles to their neighbours.

Asking the PLA what is special about those four ticks gives an unambiguous
answer: they are exactly the ticks where `vram_cpu_slot` and `vram_slot_spr` are
both asserted. There are four of them in sprites on and **none at all** in the
other two modes. Each is the third of a run of three sprite ticks. This is not
a property anybody went looking for in the data; it falls straight out of
`mpla6`.

Charging those four another two cycles of `THRESH` — the same quantum again —
takes the corpus to **263**. The value is chosen against the corpus, but it is
strongly constrained: −5 gives 254, −9 gives 241, +1 gives 256. `NEED` does
*not* move with it (20 gives 249, 22 gives 236), so the table records the CPU's
own memory cycle at those ticks and it is only the drop window that shifts,
which is what you would expect if the sprite's RAS starts the tick's memory
activity two cycles ahead of the CPU's.

At this point the raw-distance model appeared to have four classes:

| `ras_rq[1]` | `ras_rq[0]` | `slot_spr` | n | sub-slot | `NEED` | `THRESH` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 187 | 0 | 16 | +1 |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 32 | 0 | 16 | +1 |
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 50 | 1 | 18 | −1 |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 18 | −3 |

Of the seven numbers in the last two columns, one pair (16, +1) is the arbiter's
own `K` and `D`, the +2/−2 on `ras_rq[0]` is derived from where the tables record
a sub-slot-1 cycle, and only the −2 on `slot_spr` was fitted. The next subsection
shows why that last row is superseded.

### Follow-up: the apparent sprite correction was a distance-metric bug

The preceding conclusion was tested directly after the rest of the model had
closed. `class_sweep.py` drives the exact `mpla6` CPU-slot waveform, chooses a
natural representative of each of the four classes, and tests the two cycles on
either side of both CI boundaries. The result is identical for every class:

```
 mode      ras1 ras0 spr   K:12 K:13   D:3 D:4
dispOff     0    0   0    False True    False True
sprOff      1    0   0    False True    False True
sprOn       1    1   0    False True    False True
sprOn       1    1   1    False True    False True
```

Thus the comparisons are inclusive and exact on the digital cycle grid:

```
grant candidate tick iff tick - /CSx_rise >= 13
retain following request iff /CSx_rise - previous_tick >= 4
```

In particular, the natural sprite-coincident slot waveform does **not** move
the CI discard edge. This is stronger than merely observing that CI has no
`vram_slot_spr` input: it also rules out an indirect effect through a different
shape or width of `vram_cpu_slot`.

Referring these uniform tick boundaries to the CPU memory-cycle rows gives the
gate-derived rule

```
NEED   = 16 + 2 * vram_ras_rq[0]
THRESH =  1 - 2 * vram_ras_rq[0]
```

including `THRESH = -1`, not `-3`, for the four sprite-coincident rows.

The apparent contradiction came from comparing unlike clocks. `NEED` already
used `engine_dist()`, which removes the RCC cycles inserted while `phiL` is
stalled. `THRESH`, however, used raw `T - previous_RAS`. Grouping raw `/CSx`
edge times initially showed:

| preceding CPU row class | approximate `/CSx`-rise boundary relative to RAS |
|---|---|
| sub-slot 0 | −9 cycles |
| ordinary sub-slot 1 | −11 cycles |
| sprite-coincident | −13 cycles |

Breaking the last class down by actual row resolves it. The boundary-defining
normal captures are at row **1330**, where two RCC padding cycles have completed.
Rows 28 and 92 leave large unmeasured gaps around the boundary, and row 1264 has
no dropped request there. The one exceptional row-28 reconstruction is
`stop-rdwrCpu-5g`, whose pre-window timebase is independently known bad.

The correct drop comparison is therefore

```
signed_engine_dist(previous_RAS, T) >= THRESH(previous_RAS)
```

where the backward case is the negation of forward `engine_dist()` over the
reversed interval, excluding RCC padding in either direction. At row 1330 the
discriminating raw margin `-3` crosses the two-cycle padding completion, so its
stalled-grid margin is exactly `-1`: the CI value. No sprite term is needed.

With this change and `THRESH = -1` at all four coincident rows, the common-`phi`
result remains **263/266**, with exactly the same three residual captures. The
per-capture result is 264/266 rather than the old empirical 265/266; the extra
non-reconstruction is the already suspect `stop-rdwrCpu-5g`. Restricting the
old `-3` correction to row 1330 gives the same common result, confirming that
the four-row sprite class was an unsupported extrapolation from the padded row.

One extraction bug was found while following that node. For rotated-and-mirrored
symbols, `netlist2.py` applied the mirror before rotation, although KiCad mirrors
in sheet coordinates after rotation. This put U633's gate at y=579.12 while its
wire ends at y=571.50. After correcting the transform, U633 is no longer
floating: its gate is `CLK./phiA`, and it samples U632 onto the dynamic RAS node.
The cone still requires explicit NMOS charge retention, but the remaining break
is now a simulator limitation rather than a missing schematic wire. The CI
four-class result is unchanged after re-extraction.

Two possible hidden CI mechanisms have now also been eliminated:

* Repeating each VDP cycle at quarter-cycle resolution and sweeping the second
  `/CSx` rise in 0.25-cycle steps leaves both ordinary `(1,1,0)` and coincident
  `(1,1,1)` classes identical: rejected through 3.75, accepted from 4.00.
  Thus the discrepancy is not a digital sub-cycle sampling phase hidden by the
  original integer sweep. Analogue setup/hold behaviour remains outside this
  simulator.
* CI's sole MI feedback boundary, `NET.VRAM01X_IOLATCH_VDLD`, reaches request
  decoding only through U33/U55/U63 and U70 (`NET.~{VRAM_BUSY}`). U70 also takes
  R#25 WTE, which is zero in these tests, so its output is constant and VDLD
  cannot affect the arbiter. Holding VDLD low and high gives identical four-
  class boundaries. Its other fan-outs only control the VRAM data bus.

There is also a conservation problem for a simple MI coordinate-shift
explanation. Moving a class's grant-to-RAS reference by two cycles must move
candidate `NEED` and predecessor `THRESH` in opposite directions, preserving
`NEED + THRESH = K + D`. The fitted special class moves only `THRESH`;
`NEED = 20` is strongly rejected by the corpus. Therefore ordinary downstream
latency cannot reconcile that fitted class with the uniform CI circuit. The
signed engine-distance correction restores conservation on the actual stalled
clock grid; it does not invoke an MI latency shift.
The grant fan-out in MI is consistent with that resolution. `VRAM_RQ_GRANTED`
drives U362–U372 (CPU data-latch load), U93/U216/U307 (I/O-latch output enable),
and the write-data path through `WRRQ`; it does **not** drive the RAS/CAS
sequencer. The memory strobes are pre-existing slot cycles and the grant selects
CPU ownership/data movement. MI does not feed back a sprite-dependent
accept/drop decision when WTE=0.

A controlled MI differential gives the same answer from the other direction.
Changing only `vram_slot_spr` on an otherwise identical `(ras1,ras0)=(1,1)`
tick produces no change in U355–U372. It produces a later global-sequencer
event at U629 after four cycles and at `ROWADDR_EN` after six cycles. This is an
additional/later sprite-side memory event, not a two-cycle earlier reference
for the fixed CPU grant. Treat the absolute output timing as directional only:
the U633 dynamic RAS node still needs transistor charge-retention semantics.

Two independent checks corroborate the arbiter cone. `--qdepth=2` scores 208
against 263, so the request buffer really is one deep, as the single `VY_DSR`
`WAITING` bit says. And FINDINGS7 found row 162 needed a threshold somewhere on
a plateau "at either 1 or 2"; the derived value is 1, and it arrives without a
row-162 parameter at all.

### What is left: three sprites-on captures

`stop-rdCpu-3` and `stop-rdwrCpu-5g` miss a grant at row 28 or 92;
`stop-wrCpu-1` makes two extra grants at row 1264. They are the tail of
FINDINGS7's groups B and C, and they pull in opposite directions — one wants the
drop window wider, the other narrower — so no further movement of a shared
constant can take all three.

Each is off by exactly one grant and sits one cycle from the threshold.
`--threshrow=1212:0` fixes `stop-wrCpu-1` and reaches 264; row 1212 is the only
non-coincident row that immediately precedes a coincident one, which is
suggestive, but +1 is not the two-cycle quantum that every derived correction
lands on, so this is recorded as a diagnostic and **not adopted**. The honest
reading is that the residue is one cycle wide and of the size you would expect
from asynchronous sampling at the drop threshold, which the scorer models on the
`NEED` side but not on the `THRESH` side.

## 5. Defects found in the published code

Worth reporting upstream; they also calibrate the project's maturity.

1. **`IKA9958_primitives.sv` does not compile.** `IKA9958_prim_srl` ends with
   `assign o_Q_n = nor2_y;` but only `nor0_y` and `nor1_y` are declared. The cell
   is unused so far, so nothing else is affected.
2. **`IKA9958_st.sv` has a multiple driver.** `ST.gc026 <= gc021 & gc087;`
   appears in two separate `always_ff` blocks (around lines 223 and 323).
3. **The line-end clock stall is missing, and with it both line lengths.**
   `hcntr` runs 0…340 = 341 `phiL` = **1364 VDP cycles**. The real line is 1368,
   or 1365 in the other sync mode. §4d shows the difference is a stall of the
   `phiA`→`phiL` divider driven by `gc024`, one cycle per stalled tick. In
   `IKA9958_rcc.sv` the divider is a plain ring with no stall input, `gc024` is
   driven out of the `ST` interface and consumed by nothing, and `REG.Data.S`
   only muxes the HSYNC output. So the published code cannot produce 1368 *or*
   1365. This is the most consequential omission for slot timing. R#9 bit 0
   (`DC`) is a second, independent stall input in the schematic and is also
   absent.
4. `IKA9958_reg.sv` is a placeholder: the register file is `assign`ed constants
   and `Data.M` is hardwired to `G4`, so mode changes do not work yet.


## 6. Incidental findings

**The Z80 clock divider, and why `phi0 mod 6` wanders.** `IKA9958_rcc.sv`
generates CPUCLK from a 5-stage ring `cdiv_sr4[6:2]` fed by `~(&cdiv_sr4[6:2])`.
That ring has period 6 (`01111 -> 11111 -> 11110 -> 11101 -> 11011 -> 10111 ->`),
and `cpuclk = &cdiv_sr4[6:4]` is high for 3 of the 6, so CPUCLK = phiA/6 at 50%
duty = 3.58 MHz. Confirms CPU = VDP/6 on a single-crystal machine like the 8250.

More useful: that divider is reset by `i_RST_n`, while the screen-timing chain is
reset by `i_HRST_n` / `i_VRST_n`. The two chains' relative phase is set at reset
and is not otherwise constrained. That is a mechanism for the observation that
`phi0 mod 6` differs between 8250 captures (values 0, 1, 2, 3, 5 seen) instead of
being a constant — it is a per-power-up alignment, not something in the program.

**V9938 versus V9958.** The chip here is the V9958; our measurements are a V9938
in two machines. The Memory PLA covers text and G1–G7 with no mode-specific slot
terms beyond the known extra G6/G7 pattern fetch, and the arbiter cone has no
V9958-only input except the WTE gate on `/WAIT`, which is inert for us. So the
shared-mode behaviour should carry over. Treat that as an assumption; the
strongest evidence for it is the §2.2 and §4.3 agreement with our V9938
measurements.

## 8. Command start: boundary trace and stop condition

The desired quantity is the interval from the write that commits R#46 to the
first VRAM cycle owned by the command engine. The second endpoint must include
ownership: `/RAS` alone is insufficient because the Memory PLA continues to
generate the slot lattice when the command engine is idle.

The published material contains both ends of the path:

* `CI.kicad_sch` decodes the direct-register and indirect-register write paths.
  Its relevant off-sheet outputs are `NET.WR_PORT1_D1`, `NET.WR_PORT3`,
  `BUS.FIRST_DATA.D_{0..7}`, and `BUS.SECOND_DATA.D_{0..5}`.
* `REG0.kicad_sch` receives those boundaries, but implements only the published
  register-0, -1, -8, and -14 through -17 logic. It contains no R#46 latch,
  write enable, or output.
* `MI.kicad_sch` implements the downstream RAS/CAS sequencer. Its off-sheet
  inputs include `NET.PLA1.VRAM_RAS_RQ.D_{0..1}`,
  `NET.PLA1.VRAM_RQ_GRANTED`, `NET.PLA1.VRAM_SLOT_CPU`,
  `NET.PLA1.VRAM_SLOT_SPR`, `BUS.RASBUS`, `BUS.CASBUS`, and the VRAM data
  buses. None reports command-ready state or command ownership.

There is no published sheet or Verilog module between these ends. In
particular, the repository tree contains no R#46 register sheet, command state
machine, command address generator, or ALU-control PLA. The Verilog register
file has no write path, `IKA9958_ci.sv` is empty, and the README explicitly
marks `ALU control (PLA)` as not started.

Consequently the available gates can determine the port-decode delay and the
fixed request-to-pin sequencing separately, but cannot determine when the
missing controller declares the first command operation ready. That unknown
middle is exactly the dominant term in command startup. No cross-sheet timing
constraint limits it to a particular number of cycles, so neither the HMMV
startup value nor the values for the other commands can be derived from the
published IKA9958 material.

This is the stop condition for the command-start investigation. Continuing
would require unpublished schematics or a new reverse engineering of the
command-control region from the die trace; the existing extractor cannot fill
in absent logic.


## 9. Status and next steps

Settled by this investigation:

* the slot lattice, from silicon — 1368, 154/88/31, positions within one `phiL`
  tick, sprites-on active interior exact
* the arbiter's *form*: one-bit buffer, drop-new with the grant dominating,
  grant at the first run-start slot after a fixed delay from the `/CSx` rise
* `cpu_slots_of` is not a heuristic — it is the silicon's rising-edge detector
* the decision rule carries no display-mode dependence

Also settled, from §4b: the VRAM grid is 2 cycles, the sub-slot goes to whichever
half the display side did not claim, and with that the whole active-display
region of sprites off and sprites on is reproduced exactly.

Settled since, in §4c–§4f — this supersedes the "open" list that stood here:

* the line length, both of them: 1364 from `hcntr`'s own period, +4 for
  `S = 0` and +1 otherwise, from one gate-level mechanism
* the lattice, exactly: 273 of 273 measured positions in all three modes, with
  no fitted parameter
* the sub-slot rule, determined rather than chosen
* the padding: it is the clock stall, and `stretch_cas`'s rows are two of the
  four derived insertions
* the CPU arbitration rule: four tick classes, three PLA signals, no display
  mode and no slot row — 263 of 266
* groups A, B and C of the FINDINGS7 residue: seven of the ten captures fit,
  and group A entirely
